# Architecture

## Tech stack

- Godot 4 **.NET edition**, C#. Forward+ renderer, Jolt physics, physics interpolation on.
- 3D world with a locked side camera: a narrow-FOV (30°) perspective `Camera3D` at Z = +22 looking down −Z.
- The player is a `CharacterBody3D` locked to Z = 0 (`axis_lock_linear_z` plus clamping in code). All gameplay physics stays on X/Y.
- Versions and install paths: [toolchain.md](toolchain.md). Working with Godot files as text: [godot-csharp.md](godot-csharp.md).

## Design patterns

- **Player state machine:** one class per state in `scripts/player/states/`. Each physics tick the active state sets velocity and returns the next state (or null); `Player` then calls `MoveAndSlide` and keeps the body on Z = 0. Shared timers and movement helpers live on `Player`. No giant `_PhysicsProcess`.
- **Composition:** scenes are small, with one responsibility each. `Main.tscn` wires the room, player, camera, HUD and cheats together through typed `[Export]` node references.
- **Materials:** a material is a `.tres` (`ShaderMaterial`) referenced by blocks and scenes, never a shader assigned in code, so an authored material can replace it. Shaders read colors from the biome palette and the piece's **surface frame** from its mesh (`lib/surface.gdshaderinc`): UV is the position on a rectangular patch (a box face, one side of a swept profile) in meters, UV2 the patch size (negative along an axis that wraps, like a pipe's girth), TANGENT/BINORMAL the patch directions, COLOR.r the height above the piece's bottom (/ 8 m, 8-bit) and COLOR.g a per-part variation. Panels, seams and edge wear follow any kit piece that way, curved or not, with no texture UVs and no triplanar blending. A block's look can be overridden (`Block.Material`: wet stone, rust…) without touching its `Surface`, which alone decides the physics layer. Pieces behind the play plane use the `*_mid` materials (`shade` < 1), so the play plane stays the brightest layer and only grippable metal reads as bright metal.
- **Environment kit:** each piece is a `[GlobalClass]` mesh resource (`ColumnMesh`, `ArchMesh`, `PipeMesh`…) deriving from `KitMesh : PrimitiveMesh`, used by a plain `MeshInstance3D`. Its geometry is pure C# (`KitShapes`, `MeshBuilder`), unit tested. Rooms place pieces as sub-resources (pieces sharing one resource share one generated mesh). Swapping in authored art means replacing the mesh resource; an authored mesh keeps the procedural materials only if it carries the same surface frame. Kit pieces are visual only: gameplay collision is `Block` boxes (`CollisionOnly` when a kit piece shows the block, like the grate platform).
- **Biomes:** a room references a `BiomeProfile`. `Room.ApplyBiome` pushes the palette into global shader uniforms (`[shader_globals]` in `project.godot`, names in `ShaderGlobals`) and sets the room's environment, key light and lamps. Materials read the palette from those globals, so they follow the biome without per-room copies, and an authored material can replace a procedural one and still use them. Machines take their colors from the same palette (`machine.gdshader`). The biome also sets the volumetric fog, the color of the room's light shafts (`Room.Shafts`) and glyph lights (`Room.GlyphLights`), the far haze, water colors and caustics, and the finishing pass (vignette, grain) through `post_*` globals.
- **Atmosphere in a room** (M3): light shafts are shadowed `SpotLight3D`s listed in `Room.Shafts`; a negative-density `FogVolume` (`fog_clear.tres`) keeps volumetric fog off the space in front of the play plane; ambient particles are instances of `scenes/fx/*.tscn` with `Extents` and `Density` set; water is a `WaterBody` (one per room for now: the water globals hold one level and area). Visual only: nothing here touches gameplay or collision.
- **Rooms:** each room is its own scene in `scenes/rooms/` with a `Room` root: spawn point, camera bounds, biome, photo spots, and `RoomExit` markers. `World` (in `Main`) loads one; nodes that need the room (camera, cheats, capture) reference `World` and read `World.Room`. An exit's `Requires` is for leaving through it; the way back is the target exit's own, and a two-way exit's target must lead back (`OneWay` otherwise). `RoomValidator` (in `tools/check.sh`) checks every room: block edges on the 0.5 m grid and axis-aligned, kit piece origins on the 0.25 m grid, spawn and exits inside the bounds, exit requirements parse, targets exist and lead back, at least one photo spot. Room transitions come in M6, the map graph extractor in M7.

- **Machines** (M5): one `Machine` node type for every species; a room places `Machine` nodes (in `Machines`) pointing at a `MachineSpecies` `.tres`. The species holds everything else: its body (a `PartAssembly` in `resources/parts/`, the same part system as the pilgrim), its locomotion (`Walk` on the ground, `Hover` in the plane, `Crawl` over the faces of the metal block it starts on, found from the physics layer), its stats and palette, its behavior and its animation. The behavior is a `MachineBehavior` resource exporting the knobs of one **brain**: a pure class (`machines/brains/`) that turns a `Perception` into an `Intent` each tick, built from the vocabulary (`Senses`, `Steering`, `PrayerCycle`, `StrikeAction`) and unit tested without the engine. The intent's channels (`Pray`, `Strike`, `Alert`, `Rouse`, `Work`) and the machine's speed drive its animation as data: `JointDriver`s pose, wave, spin or aim joints, `MachineLeg`s step by IK with planted feet. `MachineDirector` (in `Main`) collects the room's machines when it loads, builds their perception (the player's chest and line of sight on the world layers, impacts from the player's landings and dashes, neighbors and what they broadcast) and steps them in scene order, so look-dev and checks are deterministic. Adding a species is one `.tres` (and its body); a brain only for a truly new behavior. A strike's wind-up always comes before the blow, and only hostile species' eyes turn the hazard color. `SpeciesValidator` (in `tools/check.sh`) checks every species' data and runs each one alone in the arena, without and with the player.
- **Text** is data: `resources/text/lines.tres` (`LineBook`) holds every line with a stable id, a speaker and delivery notes; species point at their creed by id.

## Physics layers

Defined once in `project.godot` and mirrored in `scripts/core/PhysicsLayers.cs`. Code sets layers from these constants (`Player` and `Block` do so in `_Ready`).

| # | Name | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `world_rock` | Rock blocks (not grippable) |
| 2 | `world_metal` | Metal blocks (mag-grip) |
| 3 | `player` | The player |
| 4 | `enemy` | Machines |
| 5 | `hazard` | Damage zones |
| 6 | `water` | Water volumes |
| 7 | `trigger` | Room exits, events |

## Rules that keep the long-term objectives cheap

- Rooms are separate scenes. Connections are **marker nodes inside the room scene** that carry an ability requirement expression and a one-way flag. Never keep a hand-maintained list of connections.
- Machines are data (`MachineSpecies` `.tres`) plus one behavior class. Never one scene per machine.
- The player and the machines are built from the same modular part system.
- All movement limits stay in `player_tuning.tres`.

## Code conventions

- Standard C# naming: PascalCase types, methods and properties; `_camelCase` private fields. One class per file, with the file named after the class.
- Namespace `TheDeep` (player states: `TheDeep.States`).
- Nullable reference types on, warnings are errors. Exported node and resource references are declared `= null!;`.
- `unsafe` is allowed in the game assembly only for `FrameGrabber`, which copies frames out of Godot's native buffers through `Godot.NativeInterop` to avoid multi-megabyte allocations every frame. Don't spread it.
